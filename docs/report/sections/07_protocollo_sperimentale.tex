\section{Training and checkpoint methodology}\label{sec:training}
Matched budget means matching successful optimizer updates, rather than elapsed time or nominal epochs. V2-A uses seed 20261017, microbatch 8, gradient accumulation 4, and effective batch 32. Each micro-loss is divided by four before backpropagation, so the accumulated gradient has the scale of an effective-batch loss. AdamW uses learning rate $2\cdot10^{-4}$ and zero weight decay, with FP16 automatic mixed precision and GradScaler.

EMA has decay 0.9999 and is updated only after a successful optimizer update. An AMP-skipped attempt advances neither the scientific update counter nor the EMA. Thus four accumulated microbatches produce one update attempt, while 40\,000 denotes 40\,000 applied updates. Atomic checkpoints are written every 5\,000 successful updates and at \texttt{latest}. This accounting is material to comparability: counting attempted updates would silently give an AMP-unstable run a different optimization budget.

H7 is the native32 conditional EMA endpoint at 40\,000 updates; V1 uses the matching native32 image family without text conditioning. The V2-A unconditional and conditional endpoints are native64 EMA models at 60\,000 updates. The native64 contract uses microbatch 8 with accumulation 4; the native32 family uses effective batch 32. Therefore cross-resolution differences also include model-family and preprocessing differences and cannot isolate resolution.

Checkpoint provenance is part of the experimental method. Each released EMA package is paired with its SHA-256 and, where applicable, the distinct source full-state digest in \cref{app:provenance}. Exact-state continuation claims in the conditional ablation study require restoration of model, EMA, optimizer, scaler, RNG, and data-position state; they are not warm-start claims. STEP~13 is complete and supplies the final unconditional marginal-sampling results in \cref{tab:unconditional-final}.
