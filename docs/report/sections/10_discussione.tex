\section{Discussion, evaluator sensitivity, and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
The evidence answers different questions and should not be collapsed into a universal ranking. H7 gives the most reliable native32 conditioning result relative to CA8, at a small additional parameter and latency cost. Common-32 quality does not identify a significant CA8--H7 difference. V2-A is a different-resolution endpoint, so both its visual signal and evaluator input route differ from native32.

The V2-A reliability investigation establishes a material limitation of evaluator-derived synthetic hair scores. The frozen evaluator is near-perfect on 5,000 authentic non-protected ID\_VAL images (native64 hair accuracy .9988) yet predicts silver in 95.75\% of V2-A samples. Pipeline parity checks found RGB decoding, channel order, normalization, resize policy, evaluator weights and label mapping equivalent between real and synthetic paths. The result is therefore supported evidence for a synthetic-domain recognition failure, not proof that generated hair is semantically correct.

The matched-budget V2-A development panel improved mean border Mahalanobis$^2$ from 2247.796 to 161.472 and channel imbalance from 15.980 to 2.798; all 120 paired images improved. On 50 deterministic final-panel images, a conservative boundary-connected background mask followed by whitening changed silver predictions from 48 to 3 and hair accuracy from 7/50 to 40/50 while preserving unmasked pixels exactly. This is an exploratory counterfactual sensitivity test, not an official metric correction: the mask is not semantic ground truth, whitening may affect more than background perception, and it neither validates all generated hair colours nor changes M1/M1.2/M1.3.

\subsection{Native64 training-budget continuation}
The authenticated continuation kept architecture, data cache, effective batch, optimizer, EMA procedure and DDPM schedule fixed while extending 40k to 60k successful updates. On the 120-case development panel, border Mahalanobis$^2$ was 161.472/13.245/2.491 at 40k/50k/60k, evaluator MAA was .5583/.9604/.9938, EAM was .025/.850/.975, and predicted silver fell from 119/120 to 10/120 to 6/120. Development MacroPSR was not computed with the official pair-specific definition and is deliberately not reported. The final supplementary panel independently confirms large evaluator and Common-32 improvements at 60k, but native64 results remain distinct from native32 comparisons and do not prove that resolution alone caused the change.
